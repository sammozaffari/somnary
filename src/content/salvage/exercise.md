# Exercise and sleep (DRAFT — not promoted)

## Item 7 — regular exercise

**Claim (short).** Regular exercise: small benefit to sleep time/efficiency, moderate to
quality (meta-analysis, 66 studies).

**Finding (verbatim from origin).** Meta-analytic review of 66 studies: regular exercise
had small beneficial effects on total sleep time and sleep efficiency and a moderate
beneficial effect on sleep quality; acute bouts of exercise had small beneficial effects
across several sleep measures. — PMID 25596964 / DOI 10.1007/s10865-015-9617-6

## Item 8 — evening exercise

**Claim (short).** Evening exercise does not harm sleep; slight slow-wave increase
(meta-analysis, 23 studies).

**Finding (verbatim from origin).** Systematic review and meta-analysis of 23 studies: in
healthy adults, evening exercise did not negatively affect sleep onset, sleep duration, or
sleep quality, and slightly increased slow-wave (deep) sleep; the one exception was that
vigorous exercise ending less than one hour before bedtime could delay sleep onset. This
largely overturns the blanket rule against evening exercise.
— PMID 30374942 / DOI 10.1007/s40279-018-1015-0

**What they do not show.** The benefits are averages across mostly healthy participants,
not a guarantee for everyone; not proof that starting to exercise will fix a given
person's insomnia. The evening-exercise reassurance is for healthy adults and doesn't
erase the vigorous-right-before-bed caveat.
