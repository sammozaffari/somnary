# Bedroom environment (DRAFT — not promoted)

The "cool, dark, quiet" story: the quantified night-noise finding (item 6) plus the
temperature/thermoregulation mechanism (item 18, added as an approved addendum 2026-08-17).

## Item 6 — night noise

**Claim (short).** +10 dB night traffic noise ≈ 2.5× odds of high sleep disturbance.

**Finding (verbatim from origin).** Update to the WHO systematic review and meta-analysis
(36 studies): for road-traffic noise, each 10-decibel increase in night-time noise level
(Lnight) was associated with about 2.52-fold higher odds of being highly sleep-disturbed,
at moderate quality of evidence; the exposure–response curves closely matched the earlier
WHO estimates. — PMID 35857401 / DOI 10.1289/EHP10197

**What it does not show.** An association from population studies, not a personal
dose-response.

## Item 18 — room temperature (approved addendum)

**Claim (short).** Sleep onset coincides with the steepest fall in core body temperature;
skin warming can shorten time to fall asleep — the mechanistic basis for why a too-warm
room plausibly hurts.

**Finding (verbatim from origin).** Review of sleep thermoregulation (largely mechanistic,
drawing on physiology and animal work alongside human thermophysiology): sleep onset and
NREM sleep coincide with the steepest fall in core body temperature, and skin warming that
promotes body-heat loss can shorten the time to fall asleep — the mechanistic basis for why
a too-warm room plausibly impairs sleep, short of a body of real-world trials.
— PMID 31105512 / DOI 10.3389/fnins.2019.00336

**What it does not show.** Largely mechanism and physiology, not a body of trials showing
that turning the thermostat down a couple of degrees reliably improves real-world sleep —
which is why "cool room" stays weak-to-moderate, not strong.
