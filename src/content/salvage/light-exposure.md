# Evening light and melatonin (DRAFT — not promoted)

The light-timing half of the "screens & light" story. NOTE: the *blue-light-glasses*
claim on the same origin page (PMID 37593770, Singh et al. Cochrane 2023) is NOT part
of this salvage — it is not in the CHK-R1 salvage list and stays with the retired page.

## Item 3 — eReader before bed

**Claim (short).** eReader before bed vs print: melatonin suppressed, onset lengthened
(P=0.009), clock phase-delayed.

**Finding (verbatim from origin).** Crossover study: reading on a light-emitting eReader
in the hours before bed, compared with a printed book, reduced evening melatonin
secretion, lengthened the time to fall asleep (P = 0.009), phase-delayed the circadian
clock, and reduced next-morning alertness. — PMID 25535358 / DOI 10.1073/pnas.1418490112

## Item 4 — room light before bed

**Claim (short).** Room light <200 lux pre-bed delayed melatonin onset in 99% of
participants; ~90 min shorter production.

**Finding (verbatim from origin).** In 116 healthy adults, exposure to room light
(<200 lux) before bedtime, compared with dim light (<3 lux), delayed melatonin onset in
99% of participants and shortened the duration of melatonin production by about 90
minutes; room light during usual sleep hours suppressed melatonin by more than 50% in
most people. — PMID 21193540 / DOI 10.1210/jc.2010-2098

**What they do not show.** Both are about light exposure and clock timing under lab
conditions; they don't prove a given screen habit ruins one person's sleep, or that any
specific number of minutes offline fixes it.
