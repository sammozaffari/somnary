# Older adults and sedating sleep aids (DRAFT — not promoted)

## Item 9 — Beers Criteria: AVOID first-generation antihistamines

**Claim (short).** Diphenhydramine/doxylamine listed AVOID for older adults, 2023 AGS
Beers Criteria.

**Finding (verbatim from origin).** The standard geriatric-prescribing reference lists
first-generation antihistamines — including the diphenhydramine and doxylamine inside most
OTC "PM" sleep products — with a recommendation to AVOID in older adults (moderate quality
of evidence, strong recommendation): they are highly anticholinergic, clearance falls with
advanced age, tolerance to the sleep effect develops when used as a hypnotic, and
cumulative anticholinergic exposure is associated with an increased risk of falls,
delirium, and dementia. — PMID 37139824 / DOI 10.1111/jgs.18372

## Item 10 — sedative-hypnotics in the 60+ population

**Claim (short).** Sedative-hypnotics 60+: ~25 min benefit vs NNH 6 / NNT 13; cognitive
events 4.78×, psychomotor 2.61×.

**Finding (verbatim from origin).** Meta-analysis of 24 randomised trials (2,417
participants aged 60+): sedative hypnotics improved sleep only modestly (about 25 minutes
of extra sleep) while adverse events were common — the number needed to harm for any
adverse event was 6 (95% CI 4.7–7.1) against a number needed to treat for improved sleep
of 13, with cognitive events roughly 4.78 times and psychomotor events roughly 2.61 times
more common than placebo; the authors concluded the benefits may not justify the risks in
older people. — PMID 16284208
