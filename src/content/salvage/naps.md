# Naps and sleep drive (DRAFT — not promoted)

**Claim (short).** Sleep-restriction mechanisms: reduced time in bed and shorter onset are
consistent with increased homeostatic sleep pressure — the sleep-drive rationale behind
limiting daytime naps in insomnia. A well-motivated rationale, not settled proof.

**Finding (verbatim from origin).** Systematic review of the mechanisms of sleep-restriction
therapy: reduced time in bed and shorter sleep-onset latency across studies are consistent
with increased homeostatic sleep pressure, but the authors emphasise these were most often
observed as OUTCOMES rather than proven mechanisms, and that purpose-designed mechanistic
studies are still needed. Used here for the sleep-drive rationale behind limiting daytime
sleep in insomnia — a well-motivated rationale, not settled proof.
— PMID 30177248 / DOI 10.1016/j.smrv.2018.07.005

**What it does not show.** These changes are largely observed as outcomes, and rigorous
mechanistic proof is still limited — so the sleep-drive story is a well-motivated rationale,
not a settled law. And none of it means naps are harmful for people who sleep well.
