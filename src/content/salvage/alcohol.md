# Alcohol and sleep architecture (DRAFT — not promoted)

**Claim (short).** Alcohol shortens onset but delays first REM at all doses; the second
half of the night is disrupted.

**Finding (verbatim from origin).** Review (a qualitative assessment of the known
studies, not a formal systematic review) of alcohol and normal sleep: alcohol shortens
sleep-onset latency and can consolidate sleep in the first half of the night, but the
onset of the first REM period is significantly delayed at all doses, REM is reduced at
moderate-to-high doses, and sleep in the second half of the night becomes more disrupted.
— PMID 23347102 / DOI 10.1111/acer.12006

**What it does not show.** A qualitative review, not a formal systematic review; it
describes averages across studies, not a threshold dose that is safe for sleep or a
prediction for one person's night. It is about sleep architecture, not alcohol's other
health effects.
