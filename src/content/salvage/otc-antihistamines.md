# OTC antihistamine sleep aids (DRAFT — not promoted)

## Item 11 — tolerance to the sedative effect

**Claim (short).** Tolerance to diphenhydramine sedation complete by day 3–4 (RCT).

**Finding (verbatim from origin).** Randomised, double-blind, crossover trial in 15 healthy
men given diphenhydramine 50 mg twice daily or placebo for 4 days: on day 1 both objective
and subjective sleepiness were significantly higher on diphenhydramine than placebo, but by
day 4 sleepiness was indistinguishable from placebo — the first objective confirmation that
tolerance to the sedative effect of a first-generation antihistamine develops fast,
complete by the end of 3 days of dosing.
— PMID 12352276 / DOI 10.1097/00004714-200210000-00012

## Item 12 — cumulative anticholinergic exposure and dementia

**Claim (short).** Cumulative anticholinergic exposure → dementia HR ~1.54 (observational,
dose-response).

**Finding (verbatim from origin).** Prospective cohort of older adults (65+) followed over
time: higher cumulative anticholinergic exposure was associated with a higher risk of
incident dementia in a dose-response pattern, reaching an adjusted hazard ratio of 1.54
(95% CI 1.21–1.96) at the highest cumulative exposure versus no use — with first-generation
antihistamines among the most commonly used anticholinergic drugs. This is an observational
association, not proof that these drugs cause dementia.
— PMID 25621434 / DOI 10.1001/jamainternmed.2014.7663
